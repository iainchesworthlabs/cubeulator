<section class="icl-section"><div class="icl-wrap">
<h2>Scanning</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>Any side, any order</h3><p>Hold a side square to the camera and the dots on its nine stickers turn green as it is read. Show the six sides in any order and either way up. <a href="../guide/scan/">Scanning a cube</a></p></li>
<li><h3>A check that the cube can exist</h3><p>The six sides are tried in every way up until exactly one possible cube is left. That check is also how it tells red from orange under a warm bulb.</p></li>
<li><h3>Corners help</h3><p>A cube held on a corner shows two or three sides at once. The scan uses that to settle the one cube in about eighty that six flat sides leave in doubt.</p></li>
<li><h3>Put right what it misread</h3><p><em>Check the colours</em> shows what the camera read, side by side. Paint over a wrong sticker, type a whole side as letters, or turn a side that came the wrong way up. <em>Solve</em> is offered only for a cube that can exist.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>Solving</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>Three methods</h3><p><em>Beginner</em>: layer by layer, in steps you can learn, about 100 turns. <em>Intermediate</em>: the way fast solvers do it, about 50. <em>Advanced</em>: the fewest turns, about 20, with no stages to learn. <a href="../guide/solve/">Following the solution</a></p></li>
<li><h3>Change your mind half-way</h3><p>Switch method at any moment. The cube is solved from where it stands now, with everything you have done kept.</p></li>
<li><h3>Stages in plain words</h3><p>Each stage says what it does: <em>White cross</em>, <em>White corners</em>, <em>Middle layer</em> and so on. The move is shown in notation and in words with the side's centre colour.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>Following the solution</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>The layer to turn nudges</h3><p>On a 3D cube that matches the way you hold yours. A move on the back swings the view round and says that only the view has turned.</p></li>
<li><h3>The camera follows you</h3><p>Show any side and the cube on screen turns to match. Make the turn asked for and the solution steps on by itself. Turn the wrong side and it says which.</p></li>
<li><h3>Out of step? Rescan</h3><p>If the cube and the screen part company, scan again. Anything already done stays done.</p></li>
<li><h3>Review the whole solution</h3><p>Every turn, grouped by stage, beside a cube at the turn you pick. Play it, step it, or jump to a row.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>Other sizes</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>2&times;2 to 5&times;5</h3><p>Scan it, paste the scramble from a timer or a puzzle site, or paint its colours in. The solve screen draws the cube at its size and names the layer inside it to turn. <a href="../guide/other-sizes/">Another size of cube</a></p></li>
<li><h3>Skip to the 3&times;3 stage</h3><p>A bigger cube is first made to look like a 3&times;3. If you can already do that, skip it.</p></li>
</ul>

<h2>Yours, on your device</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>Past solves</h3><p>A solve you left part-way carries on from the turn it reached. The list is a file on your device (the newest 100), never holds a picture, and can be turned off. <a href="../guide/history/">Past solves</a></p></li>
<li><h3>Nine languages</h3><p>English, Deutsch, Fran&ccedil;ais, Espa&ntilde;ol, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish, with the layout mirrored for the last three. Every screen is translated; English came first and the other eight were translated with machine help, so if a line reads oddly to you, please say. <a href="../guide/settings/#language">Language settings</a></p></li>
<li><h3>Light, dark and three cube looks</h3><p>Follow the device or choose. The 3D cube can be shaded as <em>Physically-Based Realism</em>, <em>Instructional Glow</em> or <em>Borderlands Cel-Shading</em>.</p></li>
<li><h3>No account, no upload</h3><p>No telemetry, no crash upload and no update check. <a href="../privacy/">Privacy</a></p></li>
</ul>

<h2 id="good-to-know">Good to know</h2>
<ul class="icl-points">
<li><h3>Your cube</h3><p>Cubeulator reads a cube with dark plastic between the stickers, which is most of them, in steady, even light. A cube with white or light plastic, or with flush tiles and no visible gap, is not read yet.</p></li>
<li><h3>Bigger cubes</h3><p>A 3&times;3 is what the scan is tuned for. A 2&times;2 up to a 5&times;5 can be scanned too, and <em>Check the colours</em> puts right anything it misreads, or you can type the scramble in.</p></li>
<li><h3>The first time you open it</h3><p>Windows and macOS ask you to confirm, because the installers are not signed with a Microsoft or Apple certificate yet. <a href="../guide/install/#first-run">The install guide</a> shows where to click, and <a href="../guide/verify/">checking a download</a> shows how to be sure of a file.</p></li>
<li><h3>Android</h3><p>There is no Android app yet.</p></li>
<li><h3>The App Stores</h3><p>Cubeulator is not in them yet: the licence of one part, Qt Quick 3D, has to be settled for the Apple stores first. See the <a href="../faq/#why-gpl">FAQ</a>.</p></li>
</ul>
