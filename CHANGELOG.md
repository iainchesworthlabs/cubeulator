## [0.2.0-beta.2] - 2026-10-31

The iPhone app now goes to TestFlight for the maintainer's internal testers, and the scan screen marks a corner when it sees one. The colours screen can turn a side that was read the wrong way up back for you, and looks round the cube on a wide screen. The Linux package starts again on Ubuntu 24.04 and Debian 13, the camera stays on a cube whose colours you corrected, and a noisy camera frame no longer stalls the scan. The About screen now says version 3 of the GPL.

### Added

- **A side held the wrong way up is found and turned back for you.** When the colours you typed in or scanned cannot be a cube, the colours screen now tries turning each side in place, one side first, then two, then three, and offers the fewest turns that make a cube. If there is more than one way, each is a button that says which sides turn and how; choosing one turns them in a single step that Undo takes back.
- **The scan screen now marks a corner.** When you show the camera two or three sides at once, such as a cube held on a corner, the screen draws a dot on every sticker it found, a colour to each side (blue, violet and pink), the way it does for a side held square. Before, nothing was drawn and there was no sign that the corner had been seen. The dots are not the yellow and green of a side held square, so the two can be told apart at a glance.

### Changed

- **Entering colours: look round the cube, and tidier side tiles.** On a wide screen the cube beside the grid can now be dragged to look round it, and it comes back to the side being painted when you let go. The six side buttons are now tiles of one size, and the tick on a finished side sits inside its tile instead of pushing past the edge.
- **The iPhone app goes to TestFlight.** A release no longer carries an iPhone file or an install page for registered devices. The app is sent to TestFlight for the maintainer's internal testers, who install it from the TestFlight app; there is nothing to download and no device to register. See [Installing on an iPhone](docs/ios-install.md).
- **The About screen now says version 3 of the GPL.** Cubeulator's source code is under version 3 or any later version of the GNU General Public License, but the program you run includes Qt Quick 3D, which Qt offers only under version 3, so the About screen reads "version 3" and notes that the source code may also be used under any later version. The Linux RPM's licence field says GPL-3.0-only for the same reason. The reasoning is in [docs/licensing.md](docs/licensing.md).

### Fixed

- **The Linux package starts on Ubuntu 24.04 and Debian 13 again.** The first beta was built on a newer system and asked for `acosf`, `atan2f` and `sqrtf` from glibc 2.43, so it did not start on either. The package is now built on Ubuntu 24.04 and checked against glibc 2.39 and the libstdc++ of GCC 14 before it is released.
- **A camera frame full of noise no longer stalls the scan.** A noisy frame could make the side reader take tens of seconds on a large cube, because it tried to fit a grid through hundreds of speckles. It now gives up on a frame with far more sticker-sized shapes than a side can show, and goes on to the next one.
- **The camera stays on a cube whose colours you put right.** After *Check the colours*, correcting what the camera misread (red and orange the wrong way round, say) and pressing *Solve*, the camera preview in the corner of the solve screen no longer disappears, and the camera goes on following the cube in your hand as it does for a cube it read right. It is matched against the colours as you corrected them, not as it first named them. A cube you type in from nothing, or a solve carried on from the history, still has no camera. See [docs/design/screens/review.md](docs/design/screens/review.md).
- **The iPhone app no longer closes as it opens.** The first TestFlight build crashed a moment after launch, when the 3D cube first drew: Qt and OpenCV each brought their own copy of the JPEG library into the app, and the two were mixed together. The iPhone app now uses only Qt's copy; OpenCV keeps its own image reading on iPhone and the other codecs it would add are left out.

> **Pre-release.** The iPhone build has been built, signed and checked by Apple on a hosted Mac. An earlier build of this version closed as it opened on a phone, which is fixed, but the fix has not been seen to work on one and the camera has not yet run on a phone, so the first install is a test (see [Installing on an iPhone](docs/ios-install.md)). The dots that mark a corner have been checked on drawn frames, not on a camera, and their colours are untested by eye. The limit that stops a noisy frame stalling the scan was chosen without recorded sessions to check it against, so a frame that should read and does not is worth reporting. The other limits of the first beta stand: the camera is proven on a 3×3 and much less on the other sizes, the other eight languages are first drafts that no fluent reader has been through, and the packages are not signed with a Windows or Apple certificate, so SmartScreen and Gatekeeper will ask before they run; the GPG signatures, attestations and SBOMs are how to check them instead.

## [0.2.0-beta.1] - 2026-10-22

Cubeulator now goes beyond the 3×3, and onto an iPhone. Choose *Another size of cube* and a 2×2 to 5×5 can be scanned with the camera, or typed in as a scramble or a colour at a time, then followed turn by turn on a 3D cube drawn at its size; the camera follows a bigger cube's turns as it does a 3×3, and the cube style in Settings applies at every size. What the camera misreads can be put right by hand on the colours screen, from the scan or from the solve screen, instead of scanning again. The scan finds a bigger cube's sides in more of the frames you show it, screens with the 3D cube come up about a third faster, the app carries its own type and has About and Licences screens, and the web version opens a phone's back camera and can switch cameras. There is a first build for an iPhone, outside the App Store, for the maintainer's registered devices. For developers, profiling can now be switched on in a shipped build without a recompile.

### Added

- **The iPhone build scans with the camera.** It opens the back camera first, with a button for the front one, keeps the picture upright as the phone turns, holds the exposure and white balance steady during a scan, and asks for the camera in the language of the phone. It has not yet run on a phone, so expect rough edges.
- **A build for an iPhone.** Cubeulator can now be built as an iPhone app (arm64) and installed on iPhones registered with the maintainer, outside the App Store. It is a limited build, new and not yet tried on many phones. See [Installing on an iPhone](docs/ios-install.md).
- **About and Licences are real screens.** **Settings > About Cubeulator** (and the Help menu on a Mac) now opens an About screen with the name, the version, who made it, the licence Cubeulator is under and the address of its source code, instead of a small dialog, and under it **Licences** lists Cubeulator and every third-party component that is in your build, the bundled typefaces (Archivo and IBM Plex Mono) among them, each opening to its full copyright notice and licence. The notices are the ones the build generated, so they cannot drift from what ships; they stay in their authors' words in every language, set left to right, and can be selected, copied and scrolled with the keyboard. In all nine languages, with the layout mirrored for Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish. See [Settings](docs/guide/settings.md#about-and-licences).
- **Put right what the camera misread.** Once six sides are in, *Check the colours* on the scan screen opens the colours it read, one side at a time, instead of making you scan again. A "?" marks a sticker the camera was not sure of; pick a colour and tap or drag over the stickers that are wrong, or *Describe this side* to type a whole side as letters (`WWW/WRW/WWW`). *Undo*, *Redo* and *Back to the scan* take changes back, *Turn this side* fixes a side that came the wrong way up, and the screen says when what is shown cannot be a real cube and points at the piece that looks wrong. Solve stays off until it can.
- **Check the colours from the solve screen.** When the solution does not match your cube because the scan read a sticker wrong (but still made a real cube), the solve screen now has *Check the colours* over its buttons, as the scan screen does. Back returns to the solution where you left it, and *Solve* on the corrected colours starts a new solution.
- **Another size of cube.** Home has a new button, *Another size of cube*. Choose 2×2 to 5×5, type or paste the scramble that mixed the cube up (in the notation timers and puzzle sites use, such as `R U 2R' Fw`), and follow the solution turn by turn on a cube drawn at that size. Each turn says which layer to move, and a cube bigger than a 3×3 first pairs up its centres and edges, which you can skip if you have done that yourself.
- **The camera follows a bigger cube as it does a 3x3.** On the solve screen of a 2x2, 4x4 or 5x5, hold a side square to the camera and the cube on screen turns to match how you hold it; make the turn asked for in view and the solution steps on by itself; turn the wrong side and it says which. A layer the side you are showing does not touch (one behind the front layer, say) cannot be seen, and the screen says so and waits for **Next turn**, as it does whenever the camera is not sure: a wrong reading never steps the solution. Tried on drawn cubes and on real holds of a 2x2, 4x4 and 5x5, not yet on a real bigger cube being turned. See [docs/nxn-camera-follow.md](docs/nxn-camera-follow.md).
- **Type a cube in, a colour at a time.** On *Another size of cube*, *Enter the colours* opens a screen to paint a cube of any size 2×2 to 5×5 side by side as you hold it, for a cube you have no scramble for. It counts the colours as you go, says which colour is over or short, says when the cube is one no turns could have made (and which piece looks wrong), and solves it when it is a whole, possible cube.
- **Review and History work for a cube of any size.** A solution to a 4×4 or 5×5 can be reviewed turn by turn on a cube drawn at its size, with the layer each turn moves named ("Right side · layer 2 · upwards"). History lists these solves with their size, keeps the solution you were following so a solve carried on later is the same one, and shows their front side at that size.
- **Scan a cube of another size with the camera.** *Another size of cube* now has a Scan button at every size from 2×2 to 5×5, and the scan screen has size buttons (2×2 to 5×5) and an **Auto** button. With nothing chosen the scan works the size out from what the camera sees, until the first side is captured; choose a size and it stays until you press Auto. A cube held at a corner is read at every size offered, as at 3×3. From the solve screen, *Out of step? Rescan* is there for every size and starts the new scan at the size of the cube you are solving. The camera has been tried on cubes drawn on a screen and on a few recorded sessions of a 4×4 and a 5×5, not yet on many cubes in a hand: **Check the colours** puts right what it misreads.
- **A profiler can be waited for, so start-up is in the trace.** Started with `--profile-wait` (or `CUBEULATOR_PROFILE=wait`), the application starts its profiler and holds, before it builds anything, until a Tracy profiler connects - for at most 30 seconds, after which it carries on as usual. A capture started by hand after launch misses start-up; this one does not. See [Profiling](docs/profiling.md#capturing-the-start-up).
- **The render thread is profiled too.** Each Qt Quick presentation is broken into CPU zones on the scene graph's render thread (the synchronisation, the whole RHI frame, the render and its main pass) and, on Vulkan, Direct3D 11 and OpenGL, a GPU zone that times the GPU executing that pass on the graphics timeline; plots of the presentation and camera-frame cadences let a capture show how often frames actually arrive, and Qt's own timing of the scene graph (the swap, the renderer's preprocess where the first frame builds its shaders, glyph and texture uploads) is plotted beside them. Direct3D 12, Metal and software rendering keep the CPU zones and plots but have no GPU zone. See [Profiling](docs/profiling.md#the-render-pass-cpu-and-gpu).
- **Profiling can be turned on in a shipped build, without a recompile.** The Tracy profiler is built into the desktop applications and stays dormant - no thread, no socket, a nanosecond per instrumented call - until the application is started with `--profile` or with `CUBEULATOR_PROFILE=1` in its environment (`--no-profile` / `CUBEULATOR_PROFILE=0` the other way). A Debug build profiles from launch with no flag. The profiler listens on the loopback interface only. How to switch it on, capture and read a trace, and what it costs idle, is in [Profiling](docs/profiling.md#turning-it-on).
- **A profile of the solve screen's arrival says where the time goes.** Three more zones inside the application of a finished solve (taking the result, announcing the solution, announcing the state) show that most of the 24 to 29 ms the interface is held when a solve lands is the screen being made, not the solver. See [Performance](docs/performance.md).
- **A Switch camera button on the web scan screen.** On a phone with a front and a
  back camera, the scan screen has a button that changes to the other one and
  starts the scan again; the preview is mirrored for the front camera and not for
  the back one. Settings lists the back camera first.

### Changed

- **The app carries its own type, and starts faster in the Latin-script languages.** Archivo and IBM Plex Mono, the faces the design names, are now part of the app on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android and iOS (the web build is unchanged), so English, German, French and Spanish text is set in them on every machine, where a machine without them showed Tahoma or whatever its own default was. Looking for the two families it did not have cost about a third of a second at start-up: the first screen now comes up roughly 200 ms sooner in those languages. Japanese, Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish text is drawn in the same faces as before. Their licences, in full with their copyright lines, are in the third-party notices (the file in the installer and the list the app embeds for its Licences screen), and a test fails if a bundled font is missing from them. See [Performance](docs/performance.md#the-fonts).
- **The graphics API can no longer stop Cubeulator starting.** The window's graphics API (Direct3D 11, Vulkan, ...) can be set for one launch with `--graphics-api=<name>` or `CUBEULATOR_GRAPHICS_API`, or for good in Settings; the application checks that the one it is about to use can start on the machine, falls back down a list of others if it cannot, and remembers an API that crashed it so the next launch goes past it. On Windows a machine with no working GPU driver now opens on Direct3D 11's software renderer. See [Graphics API](docs/graphics-api.md).
- **Screens with the 3D cube come up faster.** The cube is built from the parts it shows rather than from every look it could have, which takes about a third off arriving on the launch, solve and solution screens, and the launch screen draws its buttons first and makes the cube a moment after, rather than making you wait for both. Details and numbers in [docs/performance.md](docs/performance.md).
- **The solve screen's main button says *Next turn*.** It said *Next move*; the design calls a turn of the cube a turn, as the rest of the screen does (*half turn*, *quarter turn*), and the button now takes its words from the translations like the rest of the app, in every language. On the last turn it still reads *Finish*.
- **The scan finds a bigger cube's sides in more of the frames you show it.** A
  highlight across the gaps between stickers no longer hides them from the reader,
  a side no longer has to be held square to the camera to be read - the reader
  finds the face's four corners and reads the stickers off them - and a cube held
  at arm's length is judged at its own sticker size rather than the frame's. Over
  the recorded sessions that is 2.9% more 4x4 frames read and 3.8% more 5x5, with
  every scan's outcome unchanged. `cube_scan read <session>` says, frame by frame,
  where a scan was lost.
- **The profiling build uses Tracy 0.14.1.** The vcpkg manifest pins the `tracy` port to at least 0.14.1 and moves the built-in baseline up to the checked-out registry, whose port build turns `TRACY_ENABLE` on for the client library (upstream flipped its default off in 0.14.0, so the older baseline produced an empty client). The build gains execution sections (the trace now marks *startup* and each *solve*), `tracy-capture-daemon`, a rewritten D3D12 backend and WebGPU support; the capture tools and the client come from the same release, so a trace stays consistent from recording to export.
- **Building: one vcpkg can be shared between checkouts.** Set `ICL_VCPKG_STORE` to a directory and every checkout, worktree and repository that carries the same module reuses one vcpkg checkout per pinned commit, instead of each keeping its own copy of `deps/vcpkg` or pointing it at a shared directory with a junction. A fresh clone and CI are unchanged. A `deps/vcpkg` that is a dead link now fails with a message that names the link and its missing target, and a build directory whose vcpkg pin moved says to configure again with `--fresh`. See [Getting started](docs/getting-started.md#sharing-vcpkg-between-checkouts).

### Fixed

- **Leaving the solve screen part-way through a step no longer raises a QML error.** The 3D cube scheduled the repositioning of its guide on a call that could run after the screen had gone, which logged a `TypeError` about `stopNudge` and, in the tests, failed the macOS system walk in most runs. The cube now owns the timer that does it, so it stops with the cube.
- **The history list reads properly in Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish, and its title in French and Yiddish.** In a right-to-left language a row showed only its small picture of the cube, with the date and summary squeezed to nothing; they now sit beside it, mirrored. At phone width the title (*Historique*, in French) was cut off by *Clear history*, which now takes a line of its own under the title when the two do not fit side by side, and a date or summary that is too long for its row wraps instead of being cut.
- **The solve screen on a wide window is laid out right to left in Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish.** The list of stages and the cube no longer lie over each other, and the *Out of step? Rescan* button stays inside the window instead of running off its left edge.
- **Check colours and Scan again work for a bigger cube.** After the camera read a 4x4 or 5x5, the solve screen did not offer Check colours, the colours screen hid Scan again, and going back from a checked scan began a scan of a 3x3. All three are there at every size, and a new scan begins at the size of the cube you were looking at.
- **A 4x4 (or any even size) is no longer scanned with red and orange swapped.** A cube with no centre stickers has nothing to say which colour is which, so the scan named red and orange by how close each looked to a reference colour, and in some light (a lamp or a window that shifts the greens, say) it named them the wrong way round - and then built a complete, valid cube out of it, with every red sticker orange. The scan now also checks the cube itself: the colours of a real cube go round a corner the same way on every cube, and swapping two names turns that into its mirror image, so a naming that does not make a real cube's colours is corrected before the cube is shown. 2x2, 4x4 and 6x6 are covered; a 3x3, 5x5 and 7x7 were already protected by their centre stickers. See [Vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **The solve screen's buttons hold together in Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish.** The *Previous* and *Next turn* buttons sit side by side, mirrored, and the second no longer runs off the window.
- **The cube style in Settings now reaches a 2x2, 4x4 and bigger cube.** *Instructional Glow* and *Borderlands Cel-Shading* were applied to a 3x3 on the solve and review screens only; any other size was always drawn in *Physically-Based Realism*. They now apply at every size, with the layer to turn glowing in Glow and the ink outline round the cube in Cel-Shading, and the default look is as it was.
- **A bigger cube is on camera again while you solve it.** The small picture of you solving, with a dot on each sticker the camera finds, had gone from the solve screen of a 2x2, 4x4, 5x5 or 6x6 (only a 3x3 kept it). It is back at every size, mirrored like the scan screen. A 2x2 no longer shows a choice of three methods that made no difference.
- **The solve screen's panel keeps its words clear of its buttons on a phone.** In a language whose three method buttons need a second line (Yiddish on a phone 360 pixels wide, for one), the stage's words and the *Out of step? Rescan* button were drawn over each other. The panel is now given the room its buttons measure, the cube gives up some height for it (never below 150 pixels across), and the stage's words scroll when there is still not enough.
- **The web version opens the back camera on a phone.** It used to open the front
  (selfie) camera, because a browser hides which camera is which until the page
  has been given access. The scan now asks for the camera facing away from you, and
  on a laptop with one webcam it opens that webcam as before.

> **Pre-release.** The camera is proven on a 3×3 and much less on the other sizes. A 2×2 to 5×5 has been scanned from cubes drawn on a screen and from a few recorded sessions, not from many cubes in a hand, and a 2×2's six flat sides fit more than one cube about one time in five, so it may ask for a corner view or for the colours to be checked. The camera following a bigger cube's turns has been checked on drawn frames and on recorded holds, not on a real bigger cube being turned; a layer behind the side in view cannot be seen, and then you press *Next turn*. The iPhone build is new: it installs only on iPhones registered with the maintainer and needs iOS 18 or later, and its camera has not yet run on a phone, so expect rough edges. The new screens are in English and the other eight languages are first drafts that no fluent reader has been through. The packages are not signed with a Windows or Apple certificate, so SmartScreen and Gatekeeper will ask before they run; the GPG signatures, attestations and SBOMs are how to check them instead. The Linux and macOS builds are made and checked by CI, not yet run on hardware by the author.

## [0.1.0-beta.1] - 2026-10-15

The first release of Cubeulator, a Rubik's Cube solver that reads the cube through a camera. Hold each side of a scrambled cube up to the camera and it works out the cube, solves it, and shows you the solution turn by turn on a 3D cube. For Windows, macOS and Linux, and in a browser.

### Added

- **Scan a cube by holding each side to the camera.** Show it the six sides, square on, and each fills its own chip on the scan screen in the colours the camera measured; when the sixth is read the cube is known and the solve begins. There is no cube to build by hand and nothing to type. See [docs/vision-pipeline.md](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Red and orange are told apart under warm light.** Under a tungsten bulb the two are only a few degrees of hue apart, so a name is not enough. The scan lets the cube decide: only one naming of the colours makes a cube that can exist, and that is the one it keeps.
- **Three ways to solve it, picked on the solve screen.** *Beginner* works layer by layer and takes about 100 moves; *Intermediate* is the CFOP method, about 50; *Advanced* is Kociemba's two-phase search, about 20. Beginner is the default. Each answers in about a second or less on a desktop. See [docs/solver-methods.md](docs/solver-methods.md).
- **The solution is shown on a 3D cube, not spelt out.** The layer the next move turns nudges a little way in the direction it has to go, and back (twice for a half turn); stepping turns it all the way. A move on the back swings the view round behind the cube first, and says that it is the view that has turned. You do not need to read `R U R' U'`.
- **The camera follows the solve.** It stays on after the scan. A side held square to it is matched against the cube as it should stand, and the 3D cube turns to match your hold. When the side in view fits the cube after the move asked for, the solution steps on by itself; when it fits a different turn, the screen names the side you turned. The buttons and the keys step whenever the camera cannot see the move.
- **Settings.** Which camera to use, which method solves, the appearance (follow the system, light, or dark), and the language; also how the 3D cube is shaded and whether it turns by itself, and which graphics system draws the window. The method, the appearance, the language and the graphics system are remembered. See [docs/guide/settings.md](docs/guide/settings.md).
- **Nine languages, written right to left where they should be.** English, German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish can be chosen in Settings and apply at once: layout direction, numbers and plurals included. Source text is British English. German, French, Spanish, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish are translated, as first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through yet: expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. Japanese and Chinese are each set in their own system face (Yu Gothic UI and Microsoft YaHei UI on Windows, Hiragino Sans and PingFang SC on macOS, Noto Sans CJK on Linux, which the packages recommend; the browser build carries its own). German, French and Spanish show English text until their translations are written, and in every language the scan screen's guidance and most of the words round the solution are still English.
- **Installers for the three desktops.** A Windows installer (Windows 10 and later, 64-bit), a macOS disk image (macOS 14.4 and later, Apple Silicon), and Debian and Fedora packages for Linux. Each carries the app icon, its version, and the licence and the notices for everything it ships. See [docs/packaging.md](docs/packaging.md).
- **It runs in a browser, too.** [Try it in your browser](docs/web.md) opens the same app as WebAssembly in a tab, with nothing to install: the camera, the scan, the three solve methods and the 3D cube. Nothing it sees leaves the device. One difference from the desktop apps: the camera's exposure and white balance are not locked for a scan, so scan under steady light. It carries its own Japanese and Simplified Chinese faces (a browser has no system fonts), cut down to the characters the app uses. It is published with the documentation, under `/app/`.
- **Windows, macOS and Linux behave like apps there.** The window opens at 960 by 680, remembers its size, position and whether it was maximised (and ignores a position that is off every screen), has keyboard shortcuts for Settings, a new scan, full screen and quit, and gets the macOS menu bar.
- **Every part the app is made from is credited.** The third-party notices are generated by the build from what ships, installed with every package and embedded in the app; a test fails if something that ships has no notice. The licence inventory is in [docs/licensing.md](docs/licensing.md).
- **A profiler build for developers.** The same Tracy instrumentation as the rest of the application family: zones for the camera thread, the scan, the solver stages and the Qt Quick frame, off by default and compiled to nothing. See [docs/profiling.md](docs/profiling.md).
- **Performance tests for developers.** A change that makes the solver or the scan meaningfully slower now fails a test, the way a wrong one does. The checks count what the code does - search nodes visited, pixel buffers allocated - so they are exact on any machine and run with the ordinary tests; the wall-time checks are opt-in and loose by design. See [docs/testing.md](docs/testing.md#performance-tests).
- **The scan says which corner to show when a cube is ambiguous.** When the six sides you showed fit together in more than one way - patterned cubes do - the screen no longer only asks for "two sides at once": it names the corner whose view would settle it, by the colours of the three sides that meet there, such as "Turn the cube slowly to show the corner where Yellow, Green and Red meet". See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A cube held on a corner is read three sides at a time.** When the cube is turned so that two or three sides show at once, each side that holds still now counts towards the scan, so holding one corner and then the corner across from it reads all six. Showing one side square to the camera at a time works exactly as before. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **German, French and Spanish.** Choose Deutsch, Français or Español in Settings and the app reads in that language, with the plural forms each has and numbers and dates in its own format; before this they changed only the layout direction and the language button, and every string stayed English. All three are first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through (as Japanese and Simplified Chinese are): expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. The app does not say so itself yet. See [docs/i18n.md](docs/i18n.md) for the words a reader should check first.
- **Hebrew, Arabic and Yiddish are translated.** Choosing עברית, العربية or יידיש in Settings now shows the whole app in that language, laid out right to left, with the plural forms each language has (six for Arabic) and the cube's notation and your version and error codes kept left to right inside the text. They are first drafts made with machine assistance that no fluent reader has been through: expect wording a native speaker would change, and tell us where. See [docs/i18n.md](docs/i18n.md).
- **A diagnostics channel for developers.** While the app runs, Settings > Developer (or Ctrl+Shift+D) can start writing what the scan reads and decides - each side's colours, how they were named, how the cube was put together, what the camera accepted, where the solve stands - to a file at a fixed place for the user, one JSON object per line, for another program or a person to read while the app is still up. A snapshot writes the whole current scan and solve as one record. It is off at every start unless `CUBE_DIAGNOSTICS` says otherwise, writes numbers and names and never a camera frame unless a separate switch is on, and replaces the `CUBE_HELD_LOG` file. See [docs/diagnostics.md](docs/diagnostics.md).
- **Review the solution opens the solution.** The button on the Solved screen was there but did nothing; it now opens every turn of the solve, grouped by stage with what each stage does and why, beside a cube that shows the position before the turn you have selected. Step with Previous and Next, press Play to watch the turns one after another, or tap a row to jump to it; the arrow keys, Space, Home and End do the same, and Back returns to the Solved screen. It keeps a place of its own, so looking back over a solve never moves the one you are doing.
- **A list of the cubes you have solved.** The History button on the start screen opens the solves kept on this device, newest first, each with its date, how many turns it took and how long. A solve left part-way is marked unfinished and carries on from the turn it reached; a finished one opens its solution to review. Each one can be deleted, and Settings has a switch to stop keeping a history and a button to clear it, both asking before anything is deleted. Only the colours of the cube, the turns and the time are kept, never a picture, and nothing leaves the device (`docs/design/screens/history.md`).
- **A user guide, with pictures.** Installing on each system, scanning a cube, following the solution, settings and keyboard shortcuts, what is kept and what is not, and what to do when a side will not read, each with pictures of the real screens. It is published with the documentation. See [docs/guide/index.md](docs/guide/index.md).
- **The browser version holds the camera's exposure and white balance for a scan, where the camera allows it.** A scan reads the same sticker on every side, and a camera's own exposure and white balance re-meter whenever a different colour fills the frame, so a colour could change between one side and the next. In Chrome and Edge the app now asks a camera that offers it to hold both where they settled, checks that it did, and gives them back when the scan ends; a browser or camera that does not offer it (Firefox and Safari do not) keeps its own automatics as before, and the scan goes on either way. What the camera allowed is in the [diagnostics](docs/diagnostics.md) record, and [docs/web.md](docs/web.md) says what differs from the desktop apps.

### Changed

- **The 3D cube no longer stalls the app on computers without a graphics card.** The sky that lights the cube is now a file made ahead of time, where Qt worked it out afresh each time a screen with the cube came up. With a graphics card that was not noticed; on a virtual machine, a remote desktop or in a browser without graphics acceleration it was seconds of every core each time the launch, solve or settings screen appeared, and in the browser half a minute of a page standing still at the start and the same again when it was closed. The cube looks the same.

### Fixed

- **The solved screen's figures can be read in the dark theme.** The box that shows your turns, time and method was drawn as a solid white rectangle under the dark theme's near-white text, so the figures were almost invisible; it now takes the page's own colour in both themes. The checkboxes in Settings had the same white fill and are fixed too.
- **A cube that six sides put together more than one way is settled by corner views held by hand.** The check that a corner view matches a candidate cube was tuned on frames of sides seen almost square-on and rejected every read of a cube held on a corner by hand under a lamp; it now allows for the colour drift of a side seen at an angle. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A logo on a centre sticker no longer tints it.** The white centre of a Rubik's cube carries a small multicoloured logo, and the scan read that tile darker and pinker than the whites around it, most of all by lamplight. Each sticker is now read from a wider patch and takes the colour most of it shares, so the logo is left out and the tile shows as the white it is. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Sides with near-black stickers are read more reliably.** Under a warm lamp a real cube's green and blue read almost black, and beside a white or yellow sticker the reader clipped them, so a side with a column or a row of them could be unreadable at the pose it was held in while the same side a quarter-turn away read. A frame the first look cannot make a side of is now looked at again with a narrower threshold, and what it finds is kept only if it sits on dark plastic. Over the recorded sessions 137 more frames read (2.5%), none that read before changed, and the green and blue sides of the warm-lamp session were read up to 2.5 times as often. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **Two sides that look alike are no longer read as one.** A partly solved cube can have two sides that differ only in which stickers are red and which are orange. Under a warm lamp those two colours are close, and the scan took both sides for one, so it kept swapping red and orange as you turned the cube and never reached six sides. A side is now told by its centre sticker, and a side turned a quarter-turn is laid down by the stickers that tell the turns apart, so the scan completes. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A camera that would not start can be tried again from the scan screen.** When the camera was refused, missing or in use by another program, the scan screen said so and offered only Cancel, so you had to go back to the start and press Scan again. It now has a Try again button in the place Start over has, which asks for the camera again on the same screen: once permission is granted, or the other program lets go, the scan carries on.
- **A scan whose six sides do not make a cube no longer works the same answer out again for every frame.** When the sides that were read do not fit together - a misread side, or colours that drifted - the scan put them together afresh each time the camera moved a colour by a hair, which took up to about a tenth of a second per frame on a computer. It now does so again only when what was read has changed, and gives the same answer at the same frame as before. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A cube turned slowly about one axis and then another settles the scan.** The scan took two sides seen at much the same angle, the middle of every quarter-turn of a tumble, for one grid and read no corner at all, so a cube that its six sides put together more than one way stayed "more than one way" however it was turned. It now reads the two sides, keeps one read of each pair of sides seen (not the last few dozen reads of one pair), weighs every cube the six sides make (not the first sixteen), decides only when the views that fit leave one cube clear of the rest, and allows for a camera that runs at 20 frames a second or less, where a side turned at the same speed moves further between frames. A side seen at an angle in less light is no longer a seventh side. See [the investigation](docs/investigations/2026-10-14-scan-tumble.md) and [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **The solve screen's cube is clear of the camera preview and the move label.** On a phone, or a window dragged narrow, the camera's words ran across the cube's edge and the top of the cube sat under the move label; at 720 wide the words were on top of the stage label. The camera's words now sit under the move label, and the cube is given what the preview and the label leave it, so nothing is on anything. The cube is somewhat smaller on a short window and on the desktop layout, where it now sits below the preview rather than under it, and larger on a tall phone, which has the room.
- **A side turned to the edge of what the scan reads square-on is read.** In a cube turned slowly between two sides, one side spends part of every quarter-turn at about thirty degrees while the other is too thin to see. The reader laid a square grid on the stickers first, which is wrong for a side that is narrower one way or sheared, so a side inside the documented limit (a column step 0.85 of a row's, a cosine of 0.17) was read only up to about 0.87. When no side is held square it now places the stickers again, through the lattice they make, and refuses a fit that overlaps a better one that is not a side; a side that was read is read as before. On a drawn tumble 36 of 40 frames in that band read, up from 21; on the five recorded sessions it gains 11 reads and loses none, and the cube is known at the same frame. See [the investigation](docs/investigations/2026-10-15-square-on-dead-band.md) and [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **A setting you change in the browser is kept even if you reload straight away.** Choosing a theme, a solving method or a language and then reloading or closing the tab within about half a second used to bring back the old choice, because the page had not yet written it. It is written as the page is hidden or left now. The app also reads its settings through Qt's current `Settings` type; what an earlier visit stored is read as before.
- **Colours are named by the cube's own white, so a warm lamp no longer confuses them.** Under a warm-white LED or a tungsten lamp every colour is pulled towards orange: the white reads pink, yellow reads as orange, and red and orange sit a few degrees apart. The scan now finds the white centre once all six sides are in, works out what the light did to it, and names every colour with that taken out; red and orange, which can no longer be told apart by an absolute hue, are told apart by which is yellower. Scans in daylight are unchanged. See [the vision pipeline](docs/vision-pipeline.md).
- **The scan no longer stalls the browser page as it starts, or grows slower the longer a cube is held.** In a browser the scan runs on the page's one thread, and the first frame of a scan took a quarter of a second of it (more than a second on a phone-class processor) building a colour table; a cube held in view cost the scan a little more every second; and putting six sides together that make no cube searched every turn of them one by one. The table is built while the camera opens, a held cube's colours are kept as running medians, and the search gives up a combination as soon as an edge or a corner of it is impossible, to the same answers. See [the measurement](docs/investigations/2026-10-15-web-scan-frame-cost.md).

### Security

- **Nothing is uploaded.** There is no account, no telemetry, no crash upload and no update check. The camera is read on the device and no picture from it is saved. A scan's frames reach the disk only when a developer sets the `CUBE_SCAN_RECORD_DIR` environment variable before the app starts, or switches on the diagnostics channel's separate frames option; the scan screen says so while either is on, and both are off at every start. See [docs/guide/privacy.md](docs/guide/privacy.md).
- **Every release file can be verified.** Each release carries a `SHA512SUMS` manifest, a GPG signature on it and on every package, an SBOM, and a build-provenance attestation that names the repository, commit and workflow that built each file. See [docs/SECURITY.md](docs/SECURITY.md#verifying-build-authenticity).

> **Pre-release.** The scan has been checked on recorded sessions of one cube in tungsten light and in backlit daylight, and on a webcam by hand; it has not been tried across webcams, rooms and cubes, and a cube with light-coloured plastic and flush tiles is not read. A sticker cannot be corrected by hand yet: one read wrongly means starting the scan again. The packages are not code-signed by Windows or Apple, so SmartScreen and Gatekeeper will ask before they run. The Linux and macOS builds are made and checked by CI, not yet run on hardware by the author.
